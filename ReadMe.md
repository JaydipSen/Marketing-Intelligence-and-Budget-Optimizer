# Marketing Intelligence & Budget Optimizer
### Marketing Campaign Incrementality Analysis and Dynamic Budget Allocation Using Machine Learning

## 1. Overview

The **Marketing Intelligence & Budget Optimizer** is an interactive, machine-learning-powered decision-support application developed using Python and Streamlit. It enables organizations to evaluate marketing campaign effectiveness, estimate incremental revenue contributions across advertising channels, and optimize marketing expenditure through predictive analytics and mathematical optimization.

Organizations increasingly allocate substantial financial resources across multiple marketing channels, including Google Ads, Meta Ads, email marketing, and influencer marketing. However, determining the actual incremental contribution of these channels remains a significant managerial challenge.

Traditional marketing performance metrics, such as Return on Advertising Spend (ROAS), frequently rely on attributed revenue and may overestimate campaign effectiveness because some customer purchases would have occurred even without advertising exposure.

This application addresses the problem through Marketing Mix Modeling (MMM), advertising carryover transformations, saturation modelling, counterfactual revenue estimation, and constrained budget optimization.

The ultimate objective is to help marketing managers and business decision-makers allocate advertising budgets more efficiently while improving revenue generation and marketing investment effectiveness.

## 2. Business Problem

Organizations face several critical challenges when managing marketing expenditure:

- Difficulty identifying the actual incremental contribution of individual advertising channels.
- Inability to distinguish attributed revenue from revenue genuinely generated by marketing activities.
- Inefficient allocation of advertising budgets across multiple channels.
- Diminishing returns associated with excessive expenditure on particular advertising platforms.
- Limited understanding of advertising carryover effects.
- Lack of predictive decision-support mechanisms for evaluating alternative marketing expenditure strategies.

The proposed application provides an integrated analytical framework to address these challenges.

## 3. Key Features

### 3.1 Marketing Data Management

The application supports two data-input mechanisms:

- Synthetic marketing dataset generation for demonstration and experimentation.
- CSV-based uploading of historical marketing and sales information.

### 3.2 Marketing Mix Modeling

A regression-based Marketing Mix Modeling framework estimates the relationship between marketing expenditure and revenue.

The application incorporates:

- Ridge Regression.
- Advertising carryover modelling through Adstock transformation.
- Diminishing returns through Hill saturation transformation.
- Promotional activity indicators.
- Price index variables.
- Time trend variables.

### 3.3 Advertising Carryover Analysis

Advertising expenditure may influence customer purchasing behaviour beyond the immediate campaign period.

The Adstock transformation captures this effect:

\[
A_t=S_t+\lambda A_{t-1}
\]

Where:

- \(A_t\) represents accumulated advertising exposure.
- \(S_t\) represents advertising expenditure in period \(t\).
- \(\lambda\) represents the advertising decay factor.

### 3.4 Diminishing Returns Analysis

The application uses a Hill saturation function to represent the diminishing marginal effectiveness of increasing advertising expenditure.

\[
H(x)=\frac{x^\alpha}{x^\alpha+K^\alpha}
\]

Where:

- \(x\) represents accumulated advertising exposure.
- \(\alpha\) controls the response curve shape.
- \(K\) represents the half-saturation point.

### 3.5 Incremental Revenue Estimation

The application estimates channel-level incremental revenue through counterfactual predictions.

For each marketing channel, the model compares predicted revenue under the observed marketing scenario against predicted revenue when the selected channel's advertising contribution is removed.

\[
IR_i=R_{\text{observed scenario}}-
R_{\text{counterfactual without channel }i}
\]

Here, \(IR_i\) represents the model-estimated incremental revenue associated with channel \(i\).

### 3.6 Incremental Return on Advertising Spend

The application calculates incremental ROAS:

\[
iROAS_i=\frac{IR_i}{Marketing\ Expenditure_i}
\]

This provides an alternative to conventional attributed ROAS for evaluating marketing channel effectiveness.

### 3.7 Dynamic Marketing Budget Optimization

The application employs constrained numerical optimization using SciPy's Sequential Least Squares Programming (SLSQP) algorithm.

The current implementation seeks to maximize predicted revenue:

\[
\max_{B_1,\ldots,B_n}
\widehat{R}(B_1,\ldots,B_n)
\]

Subject to:

\[
\sum_{i=1}^{n}B_i=B_{\text{total}}
\]

\[
B_i\geq 0
\]

The optimizer identifies an alternative allocation of the available marketing budget based on the fitted response model.

### 3.8 Interactive Management Dashboard

Both outputs are available in CSV format.

## 12. Limitations and Interpretation

The current implementation is an initial analytical prototype intended for demonstration, experimentation, and decision-support purposes.

Some important considerations:

- Synthetic data are generated for demonstration and do not represent actual company performance.
- The Ridge Regression model uses a simplified common Adstock decay factor and fixed saturation assumptions.
- Counterfactual channel estimates depend on the fitted model and should not automatically be interpreted as experimentally established causal effects.
- The model is evaluated on the same observations used for training in the current prototype. Independent out-of-sample validation is required for reliable forecasting assessment.
- Correlated advertising expenditure across channels can make individual channel contributions difficult to identify.
- Budget optimization maximizes predicted revenue rather than contribution profit in the current version.
- The optimizer does not yet incorporate channel-specific minimum or maximum expenditure constraints.

For production applications, it is recommended to introduce time-based validation, uncertainty quantification, channel-specific parameter estimation, experimental calibration, and profit-based optimization.
